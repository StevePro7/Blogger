Absolutely. Reassembler (also known as @reassembler68k) is a particularly interesting developer to follow if you're into 8-bit and 16-bit game development, especially if you enjoy understanding how games worked at the hardware level rather than simply recreating their appearance.

He's an independent retro developer, reverse engineer and programmer whose work focuses heavily on classic arcade and home-computer hardware, particularly the Commodore Amiga and Sega arcade/Mega Drive architectures.

His work is interesting because he isn't just making retro-inspired games. He's taking existing classic games, studying how their original hardware and software worked, and figuring out how to reproduce their behaviour on different vintage hardware.

His most interesting projects
1. OutRun: Amiga Edition

This is probably his best-known completed project.

He recreated Sega's arcade classic 
OutRun for the Commodore Amiga, producing a version much closer to the original arcade experience than the notorious earlier Amiga conversion.

What makes this particularly interesting technically:

    Reverse engineering the original arcade game's code and data.

    Working with Motorola 68000 assembly.

    Recreating the arcade game's sprite scaling.

    Making effective use of the Amiga's custom hardware.

    Reworking the original graphics and rendering approach for Amiga hardware.

    Reconstructing the soundtrack using four-channel ProTracker MOD music.

The project targets Amiga AGA hardware and demonstrates just how much can be achieved by understanding the hardware properly.

The game is available as a free download
https://reassembler68k.itch.io/outrun-amiga-edition


2. Aonic – Sonic the Hedgehog for Amiga


This is arguably the project I'd pay the most attention to if I were a retro game developer.

Reassembler is working on bringing the original 1991 
Sonic the Hedgehog from the Sega Mega Drive to the Commodore Amiga.

The fascinating part is his approach: rather than simply writing a Sonic-inspired game, he's using the original Mega Drive game code as a foundation and adapting it to the Amiga.

This is a substantial engineering challenge because, despite both machines having Motorola 68000-family processors, their graphics hardware is very different.

Some of the challenges include:

    Reworking the Mega Drive graphics rendering for Amiga bitplanes and custom chips.

    Reproducing Sonic's movement, acceleration and momentum.

    Handling scrolling backgrounds and multiple graphical layers.

    Managing sprite rendering and animation.

    Optimising memory access and bus usage.

    Recreating the original music and sound effects within Amiga audio limitations.

An important recent development: On 13–14 November 2026, Reassembler released Aonic Demo 2.

It now includes:
    All three acts of Green Hill Zone.

    The Dr Robotnik boss battle.

    Speed Boots.

    Destructible walls and swinging platforms.

    Music and multiple simultaneous sound effects.

    Improved loading and performance.

It's still a work in progress, but this is a significant milestone. 
https://reassembler68k.itch.io/aonic
