<b>History</b><br />
Back in 2023, big tech companies predicted AI coding agents would write more of our code, eventually all of it.  A coding agent is more than autocomplete:  it reads a codebase, plans changes, edits files, runs tests and iterates on its own.  Companies adopted these tools and many treated them as a way to replace engineers.  
<br /><br />
Now in 2026, the reality looks more complicated. Agents write plenty of code, but code isn't the hard part of software engineering.  The hard parts are judgement and taste: knowing what to build, trade-offs to accept + what will break at 3am, and what will be unmaintainable in three years.  Teams that removed experienced people found those gaps quickly:  experience is worth more now than it has ever been.

Back in 2023, big tech companies predicted that AI coding agents would write ever more of our code, eventually all of it.  A coding agent is more than autocomplete.  It reads a codebase, plans changes, edits files, runs tests and iterates on its own.  Companies adopted these tools quickly, and many treated them as a way to replace engineers. 
<br><br>  
In 2026 that bet has largely backfired. Agents write plenty of code, but code isn't the hard part of software engineering. 
  The hard parts are judgement and taste: knowing what to build, which trade-offs to accept, what will break at 3am, and what will be unmaintainable in three years.  Teams that removed experienced people found those gaps quickly. Experience is worth more now than it has ever been.
