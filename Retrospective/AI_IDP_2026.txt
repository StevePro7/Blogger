I actually think this is an important distinction to make in an IDP.

Many AI-related IDPs fail because they read like:

"Build AI agents."

The problem is that nobody knows exactly what "good" looks like yet. The technology is changing every few months.

For someone at your level, I'd position this as professional research and capability development, not project delivery.

Your employer isn't just investing in you learning a tool—they're investing in you becoming someone who can help shape how AI is adopted within the engineering organisation.

Here's how I'd frame it.


IDP = Downloads



I like this version because it doesn't promise outcomes that no one can realistically guarantee today. Instead, it positions you as someone who is deliberately building strategic capability in an emerging discipline.

One suggestion I'd make, given your seniority, is to describe the development area as "AI-Enabled Software Engineering" rather than simply "AI". That subtly shifts the emphasis away from becoming an AI specialist or machine learning engineer and towards applying AI to improve software engineering practice. It aligns much more naturally with your existing expertise and, in my view, describes the direction in which many senior engineering roles are evolving.
